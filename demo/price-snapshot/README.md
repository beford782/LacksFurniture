# Website price snapshot — UNVERIFIED, NON-SHIPPING

Generated by `tools/fetch_lacks_prices.py`; mattress and
`mattress-accessories` records observed 2026-09-20, the pillow, base and
protector shelves added 2026-09-21. Observation dates ride on each entry, so a
rebuild never refreshes a price's age.

## What this is, and what it is not

This directory holds **website-sourced prices pending owner verification**. It
exists to support step 3 of the agreed workflow:

```
website → automated extraction → reviewable snapshot → Blake verification → verified preview data
```

It is **not**:

- approved pricing,
- a production input,
- an authorization to activate anything.

`incoming/`, `data/`, the workbook and `store-config.json` are untouched by the
extractor and stay untouched by this directory. The pricing contract's
`sourcePolicy.status` remains `unapproved` and `pricing.enabled` /
`displayEnabled` remain `false` regardless of what is captured here. Verifying
the snapshot does **not** equal authorizing production pricing.

This directory sits beside the illustrative Black Friday demo under `demo/`
for the same reason that one does: `demo/` is the established location CI
treats as isolated from production, and the Daybreak contract check already
pins that demo content never reaches the production pipeline.

## The two traps this capture is built around

**1. `regular_price` is the crossed-out price.** `final_price_without_tax` is
the selling price. Most mattresses carry a final price well below their regular
price (Restonic Grace II King: final **$779.00**, regular $1,099.95). Reading
`regular_price` as the selling price would overstate nearly every mattress by
hundreds of dollars. The snapshot records both and labels only the first as the
selling price.

**2. A category page does not establish a size.** `king-mattresses` returned 23
King records and one Queen. Every variant is therefore filed under the size in
its *own* record, and `sizeSource` says whether that came from the record's
`mattress_size` field or was parsed from the displayed name. No size is ever
inferred from another size.

### The accessory categories, and why there are six

`mattress-accessories` alone returned **one** pillow and **zero** bases, so
every base and both pillows in the app could only ever read "unresolved" — not
because the products were absent from the site, but because the capture never
looked at the shelves they sit on. The category list now follows the
retailer's own nav from `/catalog/mattresses` (read 2026-09-21):

`mattress-accessories · bedding-protectors · pillows · adjustable-beds ·
foundations · standard-foundations`

Note the retailer spells "Adjustable Bases" `adjustable-beds`. A category may
also ASSERT a product type for records whose names do not carry one (a base
listed only as "Adjustable Base Head/Foot"); `productTypeSource` records
whether the type came from the record's own name or from the shelf, and where
the two disagree the NAME wins and the row is flagged.

A row's `foundInCategory` is the FIRST category its SKU was seen in, so a
product stocked on several shelves is filed under the earliest one — that is
why some categories above do not appear in any row's `foundInCategory`.

Accessory families resolve across sizes with their own SKU and price per size,
which is the model the Sleep System needs:

| iProtect Mattress Protector | $59.95 | $69.95 | $69.95 | $89.95 | $99.95 |
| Dri-Tec Mattress Protector | $99.95 | $104.95 | $109.95 | $149.95 | $179.95 |
| Ver-Tex Mattress Protector | $219.95 | $229.95 | $229.95 | $249.95 | $299.95 |
| TEMPUR-Protect Breeze | $209.00 | — | $229.00 | $239.00 | $259.00 |

**This is what the shipped catalog's "From $" line was hiding.**
`data/accessories.json` carries `protector-iprotect` with a single ungoverned
`price: 89`. That is SKU `833806` — the **Queen** protector at $89.95. A
customer buying a King was being shown the Queen's price under a "From" label.

## Exceptions — 153 variants deliberately NOT treated as prices

| count | exception | why it is excluded |
|---|---|---|
| 76 | `configurable-parent-not-an-exact-variant` | a variant *parent*, whose displayed figure is a "starting at" price that changes with the page's context. Its children are drilled from its product page; the parent itself is never a price. |
| 70 | `no-product-page-evidence-category-listing-only` | read from a category listing that carried no product URL. Real evidence, but not product-page evidence, and the preview admits only the latter. |
| 22 | `resolved-into-variants` | a parent that WAS drilled successfully — its exact children carry the prices, so the parent row stands aside. |
| 8 | `quote-only-product` | `display_request_a_quote` is true — the site itself declines to publish a price. |
| 8 | `no-resolvable-size` | no size in the record, its name, or its variant option. Not guessed. |
| 4 | `size-differs-from-category-*` | found in one size's category, carries another size. Filed under its own size and flagged. |

After the drill, **238 rows carry product-page evidence** and 55 carry
configurable-parent-page evidence (the child was real but the page exposed no
separate URL for it).

## What was deliberately not read

- **Stock and availability.** `stock_item.is_in_stock`, `location_inventory`
  and the `IN STOCK` product flag are all present and all ignored: availability
  is not inferred from a listing.
- **`poa`** — despite the name it is not "price on application"; its observed
  values are `''`, `'Low Stock'` and `'Product Coming Soon'`, i.e. stock state.

## Reproducing

```
python tools/fetch_lacks_prices.py
```

Paced at 8s between requests with 30/60/90s backoff on a 403. The site throttles
bursts: about ten rapid requests earns a 403 that clears within a minute. A
refusal is recorded as a category problem — it never becomes an invented price.

`/api/rest/*` still returns 403 to automated requests, matching the PerimeterX
note in `incoming/fetch_lacks_images.py`. The catalog **pages** are readable, so
the extractor reads the Next.js `__NEXT_DATA__` island rather than the API.
Pagination is `?page=N`; `?p=N`, `?curPage=N` and `?offset=N` all silently
re-serve page 1, which would have captured half the catalog while appearing to
succeed.
